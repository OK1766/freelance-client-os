HoneyBook Alternatives for Solo Freelancers: When You Need a CRM (and When You Don’t)

November 23, 2026 · SeenByAI · 6 min read

HoneyBook, Bonsai, and Dubsado are popular freelance CRMs for a reason. They can hold clients, proposals, contracts, invoices, and sometimes payments and scheduling in one product. They also usually charge a subscription, and they ask you to move your working life into their interface.

Plenty of solo freelancers do not need all of that. What they need, every week, is a reliable answer to five questions: who is a client, what is in the pipeline, which invoices are overdue, who to follow up, and what they earned this month. A $39 one-time Notion and Excel system can do that job. A CRM can do more than that — and that “more” is only worth paying for if you will actually use it.

This is a comparison of the kind of product, not a lab review of every HoneyBook, Bonsai, or Dubsado plan. Their prices and feature lists change; check the vendor's site before you subscribe.

What a freelance CRM is built to do

Tools such as HoneyBook, Bonsai, and Dubsado are client-facing operating systems. In various combinations they typically offer:

  • Proposals and contracts that a client can sign in the browser.
  • Invoices and payments collected inside the same product.
  • Scheduling or a client portal, so the client has a place to book, review, or pay.
  • A shared workspace if you later work with an assistant or a small team.

That is real software, and for some practices it pays for itself. The cost is a recurring fee and a commitment to run the business inside that tool. If you stop paying, you usually lose the workflow, even if you can export some data.

What a tracker system is built to do

When a paid CRM is still the right choice

Choose a CRM if any of these are true:

  1. You want clients to sign a proposal or pay an invoice without leaving the tool.
  2. You need a public booking page or a client portal.
  3. More than one person will share the workspace.
  4. You already live in that product and switching would cost more than the subscription.

Choose a tracker system if you mainly lose money to scattered admin — forgotten follow-ups and overdue invoices — and you would rather keep a simple list you actually open than pay for features you will not use.
